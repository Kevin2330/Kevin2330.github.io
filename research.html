Leveraging Large-scale Satellite Data and Machine Learning for Mining Detection in Ghana: A Cloud Computing Based Approach

with Charlotte (Jiale) Zhou, Yu Hui, Peiran Qin, Yuxuan Cai

Recent advancements in large-scale information processing technologies, such as cloud computing, have made real-time environmental monitoring possible to combine more multiple dimensions of large data. There is a regulatory need to monitor long-term gold mining activities in Africa due to their impacts on gold markets and the environment. Based on NDVI data from 2004 to 2024, infrastructure and natural resource data surrounding mining areas, and millions of satellite images, this study presents a novel framework for real-time mining detection in Ghana using cloud-based computing and machine learning. By integrating satellite imagery, geospatial data, and human activity indicators, the model efficiently monitors mining activities with high precision. Leveraging AWS and PySpark, the system processes large-scale datasets, enabling dynamic and scalable monitoring of mining activities. Our time series analysis explores the relationship between NDVI loss and gold prices, providing insights into the environmental and economic impacts of mining. This framework demonstrates global applicability, offering a scalable solution for enhanced regulatory oversight and environmental management in mining regions.
